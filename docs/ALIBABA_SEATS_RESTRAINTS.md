# Alibaba seats / occupant-restraint sourcing screen

This document continues the VolksMule Alibaba sourcing mission for Prototype 1.

This is one of the places where the sourcing firewall must be strongest.

Prototype 1 needs:

- two manually adjustable front seats;
- appropriate head restraints;
- three-point belts with pretensioning/load management as required;
- frontal airbags;
- side/ejection restraint hardware required by the finished design;
- a passenger occupant/child-restraint strategy coordinated with the final vehicle requirements;
- traceable supplier components and validation evidence;
- no custom-built airbag inflators;
- no random donor airbag or pretensioner merely because a connector fits.

The central rule is:

> **A seat may be a component. A restraint system is a vehicle system. Never mix-and-match pyrotechnic restraint hardware by appearance or connector compatibility.**

---

## 1. Regulatory baseline

The working U.S. MPV classification and weight envelope make several occupant-protection standards directly relevant.

### FMVSS 207 — seating systems

FMVSS 207 establishes seat and seat-attachment requirements for passenger cars, MPVs, trucks and buses.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.207

The practical VolksMule implication is that the seat frame, adjuster/rail, latch mechanisms, mounts and body attachment must be treated as a load path rather than furniture.

### FMVSS 202a — head restraints

Head-restraint geometry and performance are part of the seating system.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.202a

A generic attractive seat with a removable decorative headrest is not automatically an acceptable road seat.

### FMVSS 208 — occupant crash protection

FMVSS 208 applies to the working vehicle class and governs major elements of frontal occupant protection, including belt/airbag interaction and occupant-protection performance.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.208

The restraint supplier therefore cannot be separated from the finished vehicle's crash pulse and occupant package.

### FMVSS 209 — seat belt assemblies

Seat belt assemblies have their own performance requirements.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.209

Final road-intent belts must be traceable certified assemblies from the actual manufacturer, not generic marketplace webbing/retractors assembled ad hoc.

### FMVSS 210 — seat belt assembly anchorages

The vehicle/body anchorages are a separate structural responsibility.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.210

For VolksMule this strongly favors keeping anchorage load paths under safety-cell drawing control.

### FMVSS 214 — side impact protection

FMVSS 214 applies to MPVs in the working weight class and includes vehicle-level side-impact protection performance.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.214

Seat-mounted side airbags, door structure, side sill/B-pillar structure and occupant position therefore cannot be engineered independently.

### FMVSS 226 — ejection mitigation

FMVSS 226 applies to the working MPV weight class and affects side-curtain/ejection-mitigation strategy.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.226

Curtain restraint packaging must therefore converge with roof rail, door openings, side glazing and headliner design.

### FMVSS 225 — child restraint anchorages

Current FMVSS 225 applies to MPVs at Prototype 1's working GVWR.

Critically for a **two-seat vehicle with no forward-facing rear designated seating position**, the current rule requires a tether anchorage at each forward-facing front passenger designated seating position, subject to the standard's airbag/on-off-switch provisions and exceptions.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.225

This must be resolved early because the tether anchorage is a body load path and the front-passenger child-restraint strategy interacts with the passenger airbag strategy.

### FMVSS 201 — interior impact

Seat/occupant packaging also interacts with interior impact requirements.

Current rule:

- https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.201

## 2. Central sourcing split

### BUY / DONOR / ADAPT candidates

- complete manual automotive seat assemblies;
- seat frames;
- seat rails/sliders;
- recliners;
- head restraints;
- foam and trim;
- complete certified belt assemblies;
- supplier-matched pretensioning/load-limiting belts;
- complete frontal/side/curtain airbag modules from a qualified restraint supplier;
- restraint ECU / impact sensors from the same qualified system program.

### DESIGN / VALIDATE under VolksMule control

- seat mounting structure in the safety cell;
- belt anchorage structure;
- passenger child-restraint tether anchorage;
- occupant package / H-point / eye points;
- seat travel envelope;
- airbag deployment environment and trim interfaces;
- crash pulse delivered by the safety cell;
- side-curtain mounting structure;
- sensor mounting locations;
- restraint-system calibration acceptance;
- service/replacement documentation requirements.

### RED — not generic marketplace sourcing

- standalone inflators;
- mystery airbags;
- unknown pretensioners;
- used pyrotechnic restraint components;
- restraint ECUs from unrelated vehicles selected only because CAN/connector behavior appears convenient;
- mixing belts, pretensioners, airbags and controllers from different safety systems without supplier-supported integration.

## 3. Seat architecture recommendation — ordinary manual automotive seats first

Prototype 1 should start with a conventional two-seat automotive package:

- manual fore/aft adjustment;
- manual recline;
- manual height adjustment if easily available/useful;
- conventional integrated/removable head restraint meeting the final requirements;
- conventional cushion/back foam;
- no memory;
- no massage;
- no power ottoman;
- no rotating lounge-chair base;
- no zero-gravity gimmick as a base requirement.

Heated seating may later be considered as a simple low-power comfort feature, but it is not part of the structural sourcing decision.

### Why a complete production seat is attractive

A high-volume existing seat assembly provides:

- known rail/recliner architecture;
- mature latch mechanisms;
- known foam/occupant geometry;
- serviceable trim/parts ecosystem;
- potential existing side-airbag integration;
- less seat-frame structural development than a bespoke seat.

### Why a donor seat is not automatically production-ready

Installing it in a different body changes:

- floor mounting points;
- belt geometry;
- occupant location;
- side-airbag relationship to door/B-pillar;
- crash pulse;
- head clearance;
- airbag interaction.

Therefore:

**DONOR seat = excellent Prototype 1 packaging/development path; road-intent seat = must be validated in the finished installation.**

## 4. Seat-integrated belt versus body-mounted belt

Some modern seats use an all-belts-to-seat / seat-integrated-belt architecture.

That can be useful in vehicles with unusual pillar geometry, but it moves major belt loads into:

- seat frame;
- recliner;
- rails;
- floor attachments.

For VolksMule's conventional front-seat geometry, the first architecture to package should remain a **body/safety-cell-controlled upper anchorage with conventional seat-mounted/body-mounted buckle arrangement** rather than making every belt load pass through an exotic integrated seat.

Reasons:

- keeps the B-pillar/upper anchorage load path directly in the welded safety cell;
- allows more seat interchangeability;
- reduces seat-frame load burden;
- better supports simple replacement seats;
- makes anchorage inspection straightforward.

An integrated-belt seat remains conditional if occupant geometry/body access produces a strong reason.

## 7. Generic Alibaba airbags / pretensioners — RED for road intent

Marketplace availability does not make pyrotechnic safety hardware a commodity in the VolksMule sense.

A standalone airbag module has no meaningful safety value without knowing:

- occupant position;
- crash pulse;
- firing timing/calibration;
- inflator/module characteristics defined by the supplier program;
- instrument-panel/door/seat/roof packaging;
- belt pretension/load-limiter behavior;
- sensor architecture;
- ECU strategy.

Likewise, a pretensioner selected merely by buckle/retractor dimensions is unacceptable.

### Durable sourcing rule

**No road-intent pyrotechnic restraint component is purchased from an unidentified marketplace source or mixed into another supplier's system.**

Final pyrotechnic components come through the qualified restraint supplier and its controlled bill of material.

## 9. Belt anchorages — DESIGN in the welded safety cell

The upper and lower belt anchorage load paths should be deliberately incorporated into the central steel safety cell.

That strongly aligns with VolksMule's architecture because the body structure is already a DESIGN subsystem.

Do not create a bolt-on decorative B-pillar trim piece that accidentally becomes a structural anchorage.

Anchorage design must account for:

- FMVSS 210 geometry/load requirements;
- seat fore/aft/recline range;
- occupant sizes;
- belt routing;
- local reinforcement;
- load transfer into roof rail/sill/floor/crossmembers as appropriate;
- corrosion/access;
- repair after crash.

The anchorage coordinates belong in the controlled body CAD.

## 13. Restraint ECU / sensors — one supplier-controlled safety domain

The restraint controller is safety-critical and should remain independent of infotainment/cloud systems.

Preferred architecture:

**impact/pressure/acceleration sensors -> restraint ECU -> supplier-matched restraint outputs**

The VCU may provide limited vehicle-state information as required, but the basic crash detection/deployment decision must not depend on:

- internet;
- cloud authentication;
- infotainment OS;
- phone connection;
- general-purpose AI/compute.

Songyuan currently offers airbag controllers plus remote acceleration/pressure sensors and states controller software/hardware/calibration capability.

Sources:

- https://www.songyuansafety.com/en/solution/electronic.html
- https://www.songyuansafety.com/en/research.html

### Local service requirement

After a crash or controller replacement, diagnostic state, component replacement requirements and commissioning procedures must be documented for offline service.

Security may protect the controller, but it cannot make the vehicle permanently dependent on an OEM cloud account.

## 14. Occupancy / front-passenger child strategy — must be explicit

A two-seat vehicle creates a special problem: there is no rear seat to serve as the normal child-restraint location.

Current FMVSS 225 requirements make the front-passenger tether strategy relevant, while FMVSS 208 airbag requirements constrain when/how a child restraint can safely occupy the passenger position.

Therefore the project must deliberately resolve:

- passenger airbag suppression/deactivation strategy as legally permitted/required;
- passenger occupant classification/sensing;
- child-restraint tether anchorage;
- lower anchors if required/allowed by the applicable configuration;
- owner labeling/instructions;
- seat position/recline assumptions;
- whether a child restraint can be used safely at all in specific configurations.

### Durable rule

**Do not improvise a front-passenger child-seat solution after the interior is finished.**

This is a regulatory/restraint architecture decision that must be resolved with the restraint supplier and compliance engineer.

## 18. Seat comfort still matters

Simple does not mean punitive.

Seat selection/tuning should evaluate:

- cushion pressure distribution;
- thigh support;
- lumbar support;
- seat-back contour;
- steering/pedal relationship;
- ingress/egress;
- long-duration comfort;
- seat height;
- head restraint comfort/geometry;
- material durability;
- cleanability.

The best path may be a proven production seat frame/foam family with simple durable upholstery rather than designing comfort from scratch.

## 19. Seat mounts should be simple, visible and replaceable

The seat-to-body interface should use:

- conventional accessible high-strength bolted mounts;
- reinforced floor/crossmember structure;
- clearly documented fasteners/torque;
- no bonded seat track;
- no hidden irreversible attachment.

Seat removal should be possible without dropping the battery or dismantling the dashboard.

Electrical seat/restraint connectors should be accessible after normal trim removal.

## 20. Crash pulse is a vehicle property

A high-quality airbag system cannot rescue a badly designed crash pulse by itself.

The welded safety cell, front/rear crumple/load paths and restraint system must be developed iteratively.

The restraint supplier needs representative or measured vehicle crash-pulse data for calibration.

Therefore:

- do not freeze deployment calibration before structural crash behavior exists;
- do not assume calibration from a donor vehicle transfers;
- use sled testing and staged vehicle testing with the qualified restraint supplier/lab.

---

## 21. Instrument panel / steering wheel / trim are restraint interfaces

The following are not merely styling parts:

- steering wheel rim/hub around the driver airbag;
- passenger-side instrument panel/deployment surface;
- seat-side trim around side airbags;
- headliner/roof trim around curtain airbags;
- pillar trim near curtain deployment;
- knee-area trim under FMVSS 201/208 interactions.

### Durable rule

**Interior styling must never block, redirect or unpredictably alter a restraint deployment path.**

The restraint supplier must review relevant trim/material/geometry before validation freeze.

---

## 22. Used restraint hardware — do not use for road intent

Used airbags, pretensioners and crash ECUs create unknown histories:

- previous crash exposure;
- storage/environmental exposure;
- counterfeit/repackaged parts;
- incorrect part variants;
- undocumented firmware/calibration.

A donor car can be valuable for **measurement and learning**, but its used pyrotechnic hardware should not become VolksMule's final restraint supply chain.

Final road-intent restraint components should be new, traceable and supplier-controlled.

## 24. New durable requirements produced by this screen

### REST-001 — Restraint system integrity

Belts, pretensioners/load limiters, airbags, sensors and restraint ECU are treated as one calibrated occupant-safety system. They are not mixed across suppliers/programs without supported engineering and validation.

### REST-002 — No generic pyrotechnic sourcing

Road-intent airbags, pretensioners and related pyrotechnic restraint hardware must be new, traceable components supplied through the qualified restraint-system program. Generic/used marketplace parts are excluded.

### REST-003 — Seat structure is a safety load path

Seat frame, recliner, rails, floor attachments and head restraint must be selected/validated as a seating system, not furniture.

### REST-004 — Belt anchorages remain under safety-cell control

Upper/lower belt anchorage coordinates and structural reinforcements belong in controlled VolksMule body CAD and must be validated with the finished seat/belt geometry.

### REST-005 — Crash pulse and restraint calibration converge

The restraint deployment/calibration program must use the actual or validated representative VolksMule crash pulse. Donor calibration does not automatically transfer.

### REST-006 — Local restraint diagnostics

Restraint-system fault diagnosis, post-crash service requirements and controller/component commissioning procedures must be documentable for offline service without supplier cloud authorization.

### REST-007 — Passenger child-restraint strategy is an architecture decision

Because Prototype 1 has no rear seat, front-passenger child-restraint/tether/airbag provisions must be explicitly resolved before interior/body freeze.

### REST-008 — Seat-mounted side airbag stays with the seat program

If a side airbag is mounted in the seat, seat frame, foam, trim, airbag module and restraint calibration are validated as a coordinated package.

### REST-009 — Curtain restraint volume is reserved early

Roof rail/pillar/headliner/body CAD must reserve and protect the required ejection-mitigation/curtain restraint mounting and deployment volume before styling freeze.

### REST-010 — Interior trim is part of restraint integration

Instrument panel, steering wheel, seat trim, pillar trim and headliner surfaces that interact with restraint deployment are subject to supplier review/validation.

### REST-011 — Seat removal remains ordinary service

Front seats should remain conventionally bolted and removable without battery removal or major body disassembly.

### REST-012 — Simple seat first

Prototype 1 begins with a manual automotive seat architecture. Power/memory/massage/zero-gravity functions require a separate demonstrated use case.

## 28. Validation plan

Occupant restraints are not a subsystem to validate casually on a driveway or open road.

Validation should progress through qualified engineering/laboratory methods.

### Seating static / durability

- seat/rail attachment strength;
- rail latch engagement through full adjustment range;
- recliner strength;
- seat-frame fatigue;
- head-restraint geometry/performance;
- mount/fastener inspection;
- seat travel and belt interaction.

### Belt / anchorage

- supplier belt assembly evidence to FMVSS 209;
- body anchorage structural validation to applicable FMVSS 210 requirements;
- belt fit across intended occupant sizes and full seat travel;
- buckle accessibility;
- belt reminder/switch behavior.

### Non-deployment electronics integration

Before any crash/sled program:

- sensor communication diagnostics;
- ECU wake/power behavior;
- DTC handling;
- loss-of-CAN behavior;
- low-voltage behavior;
- replacement-controller commissioning;
- service disconnect / safe maintenance procedures from supplier documentation.

### Sled / crash validation

Actual restraint calibration/deployment validation belongs with a qualified restraint supplier and accredited/professional test facility using approved procedures and instrumentation.

The program should cover the applicable frontal, side and ejection-related vehicle requirements and supplier/OEM validation matrix.

VolksMule should record the test inputs/results and design changes; it should not improvise pyrotechnic deployment testing outside the professional restraint-development process.

## 29. What remains open

Do not freeze yet:

- exact seat donor/supplier platform;
- exact seat mounting coordinates;
- exact belt retractor/buckle family;
- pretension/load-limiter calibration;
- driver/passenger airbag module family;
- side-airbag execution;
- curtain-airbag geometry;
- restraint ECU/sensor family;
- passenger occupancy sensing;
- child-restraint front-passenger provisions;
- steering-wheel/driver-airbag design;
- instrument-panel passenger-airbag integration.

These converge with occupant package, body CAD, crash structure, glazing/doors and formal compliance analysis.

## 30. Mission verdict

Alibaba is useful here mainly for **supplier discovery and ordinary seat hardware**, not for shopping individual restraint devices.

The likely VolksMule path is:

**simple production-style manual seats + body-controlled belt/seat anchorages + a qualified integrated restraint supplier such as Songyuan or Jinheng + supplier-supported sled/crash calibration + locally documented diagnostics/service.**

This is precisely where "buy the solved hardware" must be interpreted correctly.

We should absolutely avoid inventing airbags or belt mechanisms. But the solved product is not an airbag in a box—the solved product is a **validated occupant-restraint system engineered against the actual car.**
