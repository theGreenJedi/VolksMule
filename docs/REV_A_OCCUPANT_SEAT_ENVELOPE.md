# Revision-A occupant / manual-seat envelope

This file defines the **human package** that VolksMule Prototype 1 must protect and accommodate before a particular seat SKU is allowed to shape the vehicle.

It does not select a seat.

The design sequence is:

> **occupants → H-point / eye / reach envelopes → controls / restraints / roof / pillars → seat travel and structure → exact seat.**

Not the other way around.

---

## 1. Fixed architecture

Prototype 1 has:

- two front designated seating positions;
- manually adjustable seats as the baseline;
- conventional three-point restraints integrated with the body/restraint system;
- head restraints;
- frontal/side restraint provisions as required by final safety development;
- a local, mechanical seat-adjustment path;
- no memory seat requirement;
- no powered recline/height/lumbar requirement;
- no need for massage, ventilation, motorized easy-entry or app-controlled seat functions.

Powered adjustment may only appear later if a complete supplier solution somehow wins cost, crash integration and serviceability. It is not an architecture requirement.

---

## 2. Use a real seating reference method

SAE J826 exists to establish repeatable H-point / seating-reference geometry from a **deflected seat**, rather than pretending the unoccupied cushion surface is the occupant location.

Current Humanetics H-point equipment explicitly supports adjustable 5th-, 50th- and 95th-percentile leg configurations and is used for:

- H-point reference;
- seating-package configuration;
- crash-test positioning;
- design and benchmarking of vehicle interiors.

### Revision-A rule

The CAD model should carry a defined **Seating Reference Point (SgRP)** and a seat H-point travel path.

Every later seat candidate must demonstrate that its measured H-point and adjustment range can hit that package.

A seat that cannot hit the human package is rejected before the body is redesigned around it.

---

## 3. Occupant range to accommodate

For Revision A, design the driving position to accommodate at least:

- small adult / 5th-percentile-female-class positioning;
- nominal 50th-percentile adult;
- large adult / 95th-percentile-male-class positioning.

This is a **packaging range**, not a claim that crash certification can be reduced to three body sizes.

Use current regulatory/industry dummy and human-accommodation tools for final safety development.

Humanetics' current Hybrid III 5th-percentile female benchmark is about 49 kg and ~787 mm seated height; its existence is useful for validating small-occupant restraint/control clearances, but a crash dummy is not a comfort manikin.

Likewise, the 95th-percentile crash dummy is a restraint/system-integrity tool, not the sole definition of a large comfortable driver.

### Practical consequence

VolksMule must not be a vehicle where:

- a small driver cannot reach pedals without sitting against the steering wheel;
- a tall driver hits the roof/column/knee bolster;
- large work boots have no pedal clearance;
- moving the seat far enough for one person destroys usable steering-column alignment.

---

## 4. Provisional seat-travel reserve

Exact seat travel will come from the human-package study.

For early CAD only, reserve a **generous fore-aft H-point/seat-track corridor on the order of 250–300 mm** so the body is not accidentally frozen around a short donor rail.

This is a packaging allowance, not a specification.

Final travel is reduced or increased after 5th-to-95th accommodation checks.

### Height adjustment

Manual height adjustment is desirable but not mandatory if the seat/floor/column package naturally accommodates the target range.

Prefer, in order:

1. good fixed base height + fore/aft + recline if this genuinely accommodates the target range;
2. simple manual height adjustment if it materially improves eye/pedal/steering geometry;
3. powered height only if a supplier system wins the whole-vehicle trade.

Do not add motors simply to compensate for a poorly chosen floor/H-point.

---

## 5. Steering-wheel and pedal relationship

The driver package must be solved as one chain:

**H-point → torso/eye → shoulder → steering wheel → knee → accelerator heel point / pedals.**

Revision A must verify at both small- and large-occupant extremes:

- comfortable accelerator and brake reach;
- full brake-pedal application without leg lock;
- no knee contact with column/dashboard during normal operation;
- steering-wheel rim reachable with useful elbow bend;
- no need to sit excessively close to the frontal airbag;
- steering column does not interfere with thigh/knee ingress;
- foot can move brake↔accelerator without catching trim;
- boot clearance around pedal edges and wheelhouse;
- dead-pedal/resting-foot room where practical.

The physical-key location also belongs in this reach package.

---

## 6. Steering-column adjustment

A modest mechanical steering-column adjustment is worth carrying because it broadens occupant accommodation without moving major vehicle structure.

Study:

- manual tilt;
- manual telescope if supplier/crash architecture supports it simply;
- positive mechanical locking;
- collapsible crash behavior independent of convenience adjustment.

No powered column is required.

If only one adjustment can be justified, choose the one that most improves 5th-to-95th fit after the seat/H-point study rather than choosing by convention.

---

## 7. Eye point and natural visibility are hard packaging outputs

The windshield study and occupant study must converge.

For each occupant extreme verify:

- road/horizon view through the actual windshield candidate;
- visibility of nearby ground/front corners consistent with the vehicle's utility mission;
- A-pillar obscuration;
- side-mirror view;
- instrument cluster view over/through steering wheel;
- traffic-signal upward view;
- rear/side natural visibility;
- wiper-cleared area relative to driver eye locations.

This is one reason the windshield is being selected alongside the occupant package rather than after styling.

---

## 8. Headroom and roof structure

Do not quote an arbitrary marketing "headroom" dimension and call the cabin solved.

Reserve clearance for:

- large-occupant head/hair/helmet-like transient margin where practical;
- roof trim;
- roof crash structure;
- curtain/side restraint package if required;
- headliner/service wiring;
- seat-height tolerance and cushion compression;
- occupant vertical motion over rough roads.

The roof does not get lowered for styling after the safety/occupant envelope is established.

---

## 9. Seat width and center-console philosophy

VolksMule does not need a giant decorative center console.

The two-seat cabin should prefer:

- useful shoulder/hip clearance;
- simple seat-side access;
- service access to seat bolts/anchors;
- modest center structure only where crash/HV/HVAC/control packaging requires it;
- cup/storage modules that are replaceable rather than structural cockpit furniture.

A narrow/simple center zone also helps preserve the utility feel and makes different seat widths easier to substitute.

---

## 10. Seat structure selection rules

A final seat must provide traceable engineering data for:

- frame strength;
- track/adjuster strength and lock behavior;
- recliner strength;
- head-restraint integration;
- mounting-foot geometry;
- mass;
- H-point location/path;
- cushion/back dimensions;
- seat-belt buckle/anchor interaction;
- side-airbag integration if used;
- occupancy sensor strategy where required;
- crash-test / FMVSS-relevant evidence from supplier/system development.

The seat, rails, belt, airbags and body anchor structure remain one safety problem even if sourced from different manufacturing lines.

---

## 11. Prefer body-mounted shoulder-belt architecture unless evidence says otherwise

A body/B-pillar-mounted upper shoulder-belt anchor is the first study because it can keep the seat itself simpler and more substitutable.

A fully seat-integrated belt architecture greatly increases:

- seat-frame loads;
- track/floor anchor loads;
- seat-specific crash dependence;
- difficulty substituting a seat family later.

This is not a ban on integrated-belt seats.

It is a requirement that they prove a whole-vehicle advantage before moving restraint loads from the safety cell into the seat.

---

## 12. Seat mounting should remain inspectable and replaceable

Preferred floor interface:

- four or similarly simple defined structural mounting regions per seat;
- through-bolt/captive-nut architecture appropriate to validated loads;
- corrosion protection;
- access from cabin/service side where practical;
- no permanently bonded seat rail;
- documented fastener grade/torque;
- reinforcement integrated into the safety cell, not thin floor sheet alone.

Exact anchors are frozen only after seat/restraint load paths are engineered.

---

## 13. Occupant package versus battery

The battery is not allowed to steal the human package casually.

Check:

- floor thickness under seats;
- heel point vs pack top/protection structure;
- seat-anchor load paths around the pack;
- under-seat service/HV exclusion zones;
- side-impact structure between seat and battery;
- no exposed HV service access beneath an occupied seat;
- pack removal without removing welded seat structure.

If a few kWh of battery forces bad seating posture, reduce/repackage the battery before making a bad car.

---

## 14. Occupant package versus cargo/sleeping platform

The two front seats should not casually consume the whole vehicle behind them.

Revision A checks:

- driver's normal seat position;
- 95th-percentile driving position;
- passenger normal/rearmost position;
- seatback recline envelope;
- ~6-ft rear sleeping/tool platform target;
- cargo floor access behind/between seats;
- ability to move/recline/fold seatbacks enough for cargo service without complex powered mechanisms.

The ~6-ft platform target is a vehicle utility requirement, not permission to compromise crash-safe driving posture.

If both cannot coexist inside the current wheelbase, the conflict must be exposed in CAD.

---

## 15. Manual seat functionality baseline

Minimum useful manual functions for first study:

- fore/aft slide;
- seatback recline;
- head-restraint adjustment/integration as required;
- mechanical positive locks.

Potential addition if needed by accommodation study:

- manual height adjustment.

Not base requirements:

- power slide;
- power recline;
- power lumbar;
- memory;
- massage;
- powered bolsters;
- app profiles.

A simple mechanical lumbar adjustment can be considered if inexpensive and durable, but does not drive the package.

---

## 17. What is deliberately **not** frozen

Do not freeze yet:

- seat brand/model;
- exact track travel;
- exact H-point height;
- exact torso angle;
- exact cushion angle;
- manual height adjuster;
- steering-wheel diameter/tilt/telescope ranges;
- belt-anchor coordinates;
- airbag volumes/locations;
- final seat mounting pattern.

Those come from the integrated occupant/restraint/body study.

---

## 18. Current Revision-A status

The occupant blocker is now defined well enough to model without selecting a donor seat.

Status:

> **BUILD THE HUMAN PACKAGE FIRST / CARRY A GENEROUS MANUAL-SEAT ADJUSTMENT CORRIDOR / MEASURE FINAL SEAT H-POINT AGAINST THE VEHICLE / DO NOT LET ONE SEAT SKU OWN THE FLOOR.**

This is consistent with the wider VolksMule rule:

> **The computer does not own the vehicle—and neither does the catalog part.**
