# Revision-A wheel-end service standard

Research current as of **2026-08-31**.

This document creates a **soft sourcing preference**, not a frozen wheel-end design.

Its purpose is to stop wheel bolt pattern, spare wheel, lug hardware and service availability from becoming accidental one-off choices.

> **Prefer a common wheel mounting ecosystem first; let structural/brake/hub engineering veto it only for a concrete reason.**

---

## 1. Preferred Revision-A study pattern

Carry:

> **5 × 114.3 mm (5 × 4.5 in) PCD**

as the preferred wheel-bolt-pattern study.

Also carry:

> **M12 × 1.5 wheel-stud / lug-thread family**

as the preferred service-hardware study.

These are preferences, not yet immutable requirements.

---

## 2. Why 5×114.3 is attractive

The pattern has exceptionally deep replacement/aftermarket coverage across mainstream North-American passenger cars and crossovers.

Current references include modern Toyota RAV4-class vehicles and many Honda/Asian-platform applications.

Alibaba currently exposes ordinary steel wheels such as:

- **16 × 6.5, 5×114.3** passenger/snow steel rims;
- **17-in, 5×114.3** SUV/4×4 steel rims;
- broad 5×114.3 aftermarket wheel manufacture.

That gives VolksMule several advantages:

- inexpensive spare wheels;
- broad local replacement;
- many wheel widths/offsets;
- steel-wheel options;
- no exotic lug hardware;
- easier donor hub/rotor comparison;
- lower risk of one wheel supplier becoming critical.

Sources:

- https://www.alibaba.com/steel-wheel-rims-16-inch-suppliers.html
- https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/17-Inch-5x114-3-Steel-Wheel_60720108791.html

## 3. Why this does not select the hub

Wheel PCD is only one hub interface.

Still open:

- hub center bore/pilot diameter;
- wheel-stud diameter/length/knurl;
- driven-hub spline;
- bearing size/load capacity;
- knuckle bolt pattern;
- flange offset;
- ABS encoder geometry;
- rotor pilot/hat geometry;
- final wheel offset;
- final wheel width;
- final wheel load rating.

A hub that happens to be 5×114.3 is not automatically suitable.

---

## 4. Center bore remains open intentionally

Do **not** standardize the hub pilot yet merely to match one donor wheel family.

Common 5×114.3 platforms use different center bores.

The eventual priority is:

1. mechanically correct hub/bearing;
2. correct driven spline/loads;
3. correct brake/knuckle geometry;
4. then wheel pilot compatibility.

Aftermarket wheels with a sufficiently large center bore and proper hub-centric rings can bridge wheel-side bore differences where appropriate.

The vehicle hub itself should remain mechanically sound rather than being distorted to match a convenient wheel.

---

## 5. M12×1.5 preference

M12×1.5 lug hardware is common in the same general Japanese/Asian passenger/SUV ecosystem and is broadly available locally.

Benefits:

- ordinary lug nuts;
- ordinary wheel studs;
- familiar service tools;
- abundant replacement choices.

Still verify final:

- stud tensile class/material;
- knurl and press fit;
- usable thread engagement;
- lug-seat geometry;
- wheel thickness;
- spacer policy if any;
- torque specification;
- fatigue/load validation.

### Rule

> **Never use an adapter/spacer merely to save an otherwise bad hub choice.**

A properly engineered hub/wheel interface is preferred.

---

## 6. 16-inch versus 17-inch wheel

Current order remains:

1. **try 16-inch first**;
2. move to **17-inch if the validated brake/knuckle package requires it**.

Alibaba already supplies plain steel wheels in both diameters with 5×114.3 patterns.

### Why 16 remains attractive

- more tire sidewall;
- lighter/cheaper wheel options;
- good tall/narrow tire choices;
- cheaper full-size spare;
- better rough-road tolerance.

### Why 17 remains available

Revision A carries up to roughly a **320-mm front rotor conflict envelope**.

Caliper shape—not rotor diameter alone—will determine whether a given 16-in wheel clears.

Therefore:

> **No wheel diameter freeze before a physical/CAD brake-clearance check.**

---

## 7. Tire envelope interaction

The existing tire preference remains:

- approximately **28–34 in overall diameter** study envelope;
- relatively tall/narrow shape;
- substantial sidewall;
- common North-American tire size;
- full-size spare.

A 5×114.3 steel-wheel ecosystem supports that philosophy well if the chosen tire has adequate load index and the wheel has the required load rating.

Width remains secondary to:

- load capacity;
- rolling diameter;
- snow/loose-surface behavior;
- steering clearance;
- brake clearance;
- replacement availability.

---

## 8. Alibaba wheel sourcing rule

Alibaba may be excellent for:

- prototype steel wheels;
- spare wheels;
- later low-cost production wheels;
- drawing/custom offset work if genuinely necessary.

But wheel certification/quality claims must be traced to the exact manufacturer and product.

Require for a road-intent wheel:

- manufacturer identity;
- material/specification;
- rated load;
- runout;
- fatigue/impact test evidence;
- coating/corrosion evidence;
- exact PCD/center-bore/offset dimensions;
- traceability.

A listing that merely says **DOT** is not engineering proof.

---

## 9. Local replacement wins the architecture

Even if imported wheels cost less initially, the final interface should permit a driver to obtain a suitable replacement wheel without waiting for ocean freight.

The ideal outcome is:

- ordinary 5×114.3 wheel;
- ordinary lug nuts/studs;
- common 16- or 17-in diameter;
- common tire size;
- full-size spare;
- multiple local and online sources.

This is exactly the sort of boring standardization VolksMule should exploit.

---

## 10. Current status

> **PREFERRED STUDY: 5×114.3 PCD + M12×1.5 lug hardware**
>
> **WHEEL DIAMETER: 16 in first, 17 in if brake clearance requires**
>
> **CENTER BORE / OFFSET / HUB SPLINE / BEARING: OPEN**

### Veto conditions

Move away from 5×114.3 only if a clearly better hub/knuckle/brake/e-axle interface provides enough whole-vehicle benefit to justify losing the enormous service ecosystem.

This is a preference with a high burden of proof for change—not a hard requirement.
